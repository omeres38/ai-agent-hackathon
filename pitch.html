01 · The problem

AI can act. Payments remain human-centric.

As AI agents use more APIs, data, compute, and digital services, they need a bounded way to pay for resources. Unrestricted wallet access is risky; manually approving every small transaction limits autonomy.

Too much accessGiving software open-ended control over funds increases risk.
Too much frictionManual review for every payment slows useful agent workflows.
02 · The solution

Programmable payment policies.

AgenticPay is designed around a simple rule: an agent can request a payment, but its owner's policies determine whether the request is allowed.

1 · DefineOwner sets daily and per-payment limits.
2 · RequestAgent specifies recipient, amount, and purpose.
3 · EvaluatePolicy checks limits and recipient status.
4 · SettleFuture version can settle on-chain after approval.
03 · Current prototype

A focused first test.

Configurable limitsChange daily allowance and maximum transaction amount.
Recipient policyReject payment requests for unverified recipients.
Explainable decisionEach request shows why it passed or failed local rules.
Decision historyView simulated approvals, rejections, and daily allowance.

Current implementation is a browser simulation. No wallet, live AI model, Solana transaction, or real USDC transfer is implemented.

04 · Why blockchain?

Transparent settlement — when ready.

Solana and USDC are the proposed first settlement path because the product needs programmable transactions and a low-friction digital payment asset. The next step is a devnet-only adapter after the policy layer, tests, and permissions are strengthened.

NowLocal policy simulation and payment decision UX.
NextTested policy API, wallet connectivity, and Solana devnet integration.
05 · Vision
AI agents should be able to buy resources — without receiving unrestricted access to money.

AgenticPay aims to become a safety and policy layer for agent-to-service and agent-to-agent payments.

This is an early prototype and product hypothesis. Production use would require independent security review, robust permissioning, and real transaction testing.
